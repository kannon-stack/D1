# Direction: A · Contour

## Concept
**Every roast is a climb.** A roaster reads bean temperature over time the way a hiker reads an elevation profile: a drop from the charge into the turning-point col, a long ascent through drying and Maillard, a steep last pitch through first crack, and the summit at the drop. Northbound draws each batch's real data as terrain, so every bag comes with its map. The page is a survey sheet for batch 214. The hero map is not decoration: it is computed from the roast (`assets/contours.mjs`), and along the dashed section line A–A′ the ground's height *is* the bean temperature. In `roast-curve` the page proves it by constructing the cross-section the way surveyors do by hand: tick each contour crossing on a paper strip, drop each tick to its height, and join the points. What appears is batch 214's roast curve. Name, outdoorsy personality and differentiator become one image. Warmth comes from paper, sienna ink and the plain voice; precision comes from labelled numbers and hairlines.

## Composition principles
1. **The land is still; only the survey moves.** Terrain never animates. Only survey marks move (the section line, spot heights, ticks, projection lines, the plotted curve, waypoints), at pen-plotter pace.
2. **Every line is data or a rule.** A contour is an isotherm of batch 214; a hairline is a grid or table rule. No decorative strokes, blobs, textures or "topo wallpaper" anywhere: if a line isn't generated from the data or part of the grid, it doesn't ship.
3. **Type sits in clearings.** No contour, gridline or curve passes under running text. The hero headline has a meadow computed into the terrain; everywhere else, text sits on paper or on a surface label.
4. **One family; width is the hierarchy.** Archivo at wdth 125 for titles (the survey-sheet title), 100 for reading, 75 for map lettering (labels, data, spot heights). Weight stays light to regular; contrast comes from width and size.
5. **The grid holds the copy; the map runs under it.** 12 columns carry every word. The terrain (hero) and the section (roast-curve) are the two planned full-bleed breaks; the roasts' shared temperature gridlines are the third.
6. **Labels go on the thing.** Direct labels at points and line ends with short leaders; no legends and no tooltips needed to read the data (OWID, Artisan technique).

## Do
- Label every number with its unit and its event code (`FC 8:50 · 196°`). Times are `m:ss`, temperatures `°` (°C spelled out once per section in the legend).
- Use tabular figures (`num`) for every time, temperature, date, batch number and price.
- Give each tint exactly one job: moss = drying phase + selected plan; lake = cooling; sienna tint = Maillard.
- Keep the accent scarce in UI: one filled button per viewport; everything else accent is line work (contours, curve, route).
- Set map lettering (eyebrows, spot heights, axis labels) in `width-map` uppercase or numerals with 0.06em tracking. Set origin names in condensed italic, as a topo sheet letters place names.
- Draw the route schedule as a survey route: a dashed line, chainage ticks per week, waypoint pins.
- Keep corners square (radius 0) and use no shadows. Depth comes from a surface one step lighter (light) or raised (dark) plus a 1px hairline.
- Compose 375px on purpose: the map crops to the summit, roasts become a comparison plate plus cards, and the route schedule turns vertical.

## Don't
- No photography, no bean or latte imagery, no kraft paper, chalk, stamps, badges or "Est. 20XX".
- No contour pattern that isn't generated by `contours.mjs`. Never tile, rotate or recolour the terrain as a background in other sections.
- No second typeface, no mono, no serif. No faux italics, no all-caps at wdth 125 for running text (wordmark only).
- No gradients, glows, blur, glass or noise. No drop shadows, including on the route card and form.
- No bouncing, springy overshoot, parallax on the terrain, or fades on every element. Text blocks appear in place.
- No pure black or white: ink is `oklch(0.26 0.02 60)`, paper is `oklch(0.97 0.011 85)`.
- Don't centre-align section copy. Everything hangs from the left edge of its column span; numbers right-align in tables.
- Don't use the accent for errors (danger is its own token) or for body text.

## Signature moments by section
| Section | Moment | Size |
|---|---|---|
| `hero` | The survey of the map. The terrain is already there on first paint. The dashed section line A–A′ is plotted across it left to right (1.4 s), and spot heights pin as the pen passes them: 88°, 152°, 196°, ▲ 207°. A map legend states the rule: *contour interval 4 °C · height = bean temperature*. | medium (sets up the big one) |
| `roast-curve` | **Section A–A′.** A scroll-pinned stage builds the cross-section by the surveyor's paper-strip method. (1) The straightened band of map along A–A′ appears with its dashed line. (2) A tick lands on every contour crossing: 77 of them, dense where the charge fall is steep. (3) Projection lines drop from the index crossings to their heights. (4) The plotter draws batch 214 through those points while a plumb cursor reads `T 8:50 · 196.0°`. (5) Waypoints pin: Charge, TP, DE, FC, Drop. (6) The phase bar fills 42 / 41 / 17 %. The terrain was data all along. | **big** |
| `roasts` | Three route cards on one shared temperature axis. The 200° / 150° / 100° gridlines run edge to edge through all three cards, so the curves compare at a glance. Each roast-level marker draws out along the same scale. | small |
| `how-it-works` | The route schedule. Choose a cadence and the delivery waypoints re-pin along 12 weeks of chainage. Tap a waypoint to skip it: the pin goes hollow, its date is struck through, and the readout says when the next bag arrives. | medium (interactive) |
| `proof` | The valley: the longest stretch of open paper on the page. One quote, then a single figure with a surveyor's leader line to its caption. | small |
| `subscribe` | The selected plan takes the moss tint, like vegetation on the map. Submitting plots a hairline across the button, and success pins a ▲ beside the first roast date. | small |
| `footer` | The map collar: a sheet-margin line (sheet number, contour interval, "projection: time × temperature") above a large wide wordmark. | small |
