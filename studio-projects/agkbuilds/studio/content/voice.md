# Voice: Agkbuilds

Concept: **Working Drawing.** Every build starts as a drawing, and the page is the engineering sheet. The voice is the voice of a good drawing: every line is there because it measures or labels something, and nothing is shaded in for effect.

Brief adjectives: **bold**, **futuristic**, **dependable**. Never: a generic corporate template.

## Traits

| Trait | Means | This | Not that |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact | Names the thing and the step. One concrete noun beats three adjectives, the way a dimension line beats "roughly this wide". | "Software that brings back files and data that were deleted, lost or damaged." | "Powerful data solutions for every need." |
| | | "We plan it, build it and hand over software that fits the way your business works." | "End-to-end delivery of tailored digital experiences." |
| Bold | Short declaratives and plain imperatives. Large statements about the work, never about ourselves. | "Tell us what to build." | "We would love to hear from you about your potential project needs." |
| | | "Custom software, drawn to fit. Then built." | "Custom software that may suit your business." |
| Dependable | Calm and plain. Says what happens next and never promises what a drawing cannot show. | "Questions come before code." | "Guaranteed delivery, on time and on budget." |
| | | "Something else in mind? Describe it and we will tell you whether it is a fit." | "We can build anything, however complex." |

The futuristic part is not a trait of the words. It lives in the sheet itself: the drawn-then-solid headline, the mono annotations, the precision. The words stay plain so the design can be bold.

## Use

Concrete nouns and verbs from a drafting table and a build:

- drawing, sheet, plan, fit, outline, solid, detail, dimension, parts list, title block, figure
- draw, plan, build, hand over, describe, fit, measure
- software, tool, game, bot, data, files, rules, commands, server, code
- you, your business, the work you already do, the job

Mono annotations use the drawing-sheet vocabulary: SHEET 1/4, FIG. 01, PARTS LIST, STEP 1/4, DRAWN FOR, NEXT STEP, END OF DRAWING.

Primary call to action, identical everywhere: **Start a conversation**.

## Avoid

- The studio banned list: revolutionary, seamless(ly), unlock, elevate, leverage, cutting-edge, game-changer, next-level, world-class, best-in-class, innovative, empower, synergy, robust, journey (as a metaphor), "in today's fast-paced world", "look no further", "we're passionate about", "solutions" used alone, and any exclamation mark.
- Software-agency clichés: digital transformation, tailored solutions, end-to-end, bespoke, turnkey, state-of-the-art, scalable, agile, holistic, streamline, ecosystem, stakeholders, "your trusted partner", "AI-powered", "tech-savvy".
- **Claim words (facts policy, decisions #9-#13).** Agkbuilds is a real company and the only supplied facts are listed below. Never write: trusted by, proven, award-winning, years of experience, our team of, expert(s), experienced, leading, top-rated, fast, affordable, on time, on budget, guaranteed, "clients across…", "projects delivered", any figure, price, timeframe, location, client, result or testimonial.
- Self-praise of any kind. The page never calls Agkbuilds dependable, bold or precise. It sounds that way.
- "I". Use "we" only as the plain company voice with nothing attached (no team size, no history, no headcount).
- Em dashes. Use a colon, a full stop or a comma.
- Words that describe the three products beyond what the kind of software is in general (no features, formats, success rates, player counts, user numbers).

## Grammar and rhythm

- **Person:** "we" for Agkbuilds (company voice only) and "you" for the reader. Headings and labels often drop the pronoun.
- **Sentences:** short. Most are 6 to 14 words; a second sentence is allowed when it adds a step or a limit, not a flourish. One idea per sentence.
- **Contractions:** avoid in headlines and labels. Rare in body copy; the precise voice prefers "we will" and "it is".
- **Headings:** written in sentence case. The display face (Archivo, wide, 800) sets them in uppercase with CSS, so the source text stays readable for screen readers and for copy and paste. A heading that is a full sentence ends with a full stop; a label heading ("What we build") does not. No exclamation marks anywhere on the page.
- **Mono annotations:** written in uppercase in the source (they are drawing labels, not sentences) with no end punctuation. Annotations that sit inside the SVG are decorative and `aria-hidden`; real text always repeats the meaning.
- **Numbers:** numerals only for sheet and step order (1/4, 01) and for the phone number, with hyphens (254-231-1149) and tabular figures. Never a number that implies a claim.
- **Punctuation habits:** the full stop is the line end of the sheet. Colons introduce a list or a definition. Semicolons at most once on the page.
- **Capitalisation:** "Agkbuilds" in running text. "AGK" with "builds" only in the wordmark.
